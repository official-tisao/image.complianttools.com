Dear Festus,

I am building a free, open-source, browser-based image editing tool. Before I invest in a handful of
its features, I would like a screening opinion on whether I can safely implement them.

Some context on why the ask is narrow. The tool runs entirely in the user's browser — no image data
reaches any server I operate. I have already completed an internal copyright and software-licensing
audit: every third-party dependency has been checked against a permissive-licence allowlist,
copyleft dependencies have been removed, and the check runs automatically on every build. I am
reasonably confident on that side.

What I cannot assess myself is patent exposure. Four of the image-processing techniques I would like
to use originate in academic and corporate research from the 2000s, and patent applications were
filed around several of them. I have read no claims and I am asserting nothing about their status —
that assessment is what I am asking for. There is also one trademark question about naming
size presets after the social platforms they target.

I have attached a packet setting out all five items. For each one it describes what the technique
does in plain terms, what I would use it for, what I know about its origin, and — importantly — the
alternative I will ship if the answer is no. **Every one of those alternatives is already built and
working.** Nothing here is blocking me. A "no" on any item costs me some quality on one feature, not
the feature itself, so please do not feel pressure toward a favourable answer.

Three things I would ask you to address up front, before the substantive items:

1. Which jurisdictions you can opine on, and which you consider material here. The site is reachable
   worldwide, and I would rather scope this correctly than over-buy.
2. Whether software patent screening is within your practice. If it is not, I would genuinely rather
   be referred on than receive a hedged answer.
3. An estimate of cost and turnaround for whatever scope of work you consider appropriate.

There is no deadline on this. I would rather have a considered answer than a fast one.

Thank you for your time.

[Your name]
